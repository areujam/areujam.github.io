Research moves quickly, but reading can become strangely invisible. Papers accumulate in tabs, useful ideas get buried in notes, and the connection between what I read and what I build is easy to lose.

This research log is my way of changing that.

## The weekly format

My recurring entry will be **10 Papers of My Week**. It is deliberately simple:

1. ten papers I found useful;
2. one clear reason each paper was worth my time;
3. a closer look at the idea that stayed with me;
4. one connection to my work in machine learning systems, computational biology, or scientific computing.

I’ll also use this space for shorter build notes: distributed-training experiments, reproducible genomics workflows, poster walkthroughs, and the occasional failure that taught me more than a clean result.

## Why publish it?

Writing forces a different kind of understanding. If I cannot explain why a paper matters, what assumptions it makes, or where its method might break, I probably have not finished reading it.

The goal is not to produce a perfect literature review every week. The goal is to leave a compact, honest record of what I learned—and to make that record useful to someone else.
